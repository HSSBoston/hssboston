### Hi there 👋

I am a teenage girl who loves coding, music, stargazing, and playing tennis. My coding journey started before I fully understood that I was coding. As a kindergartner, I played with Lego WeDo and [Scratch](https://scratch.mit.edu/users/hssboston/), mostly because they felt like building and storytelling at the same time. You can see some of my early Lego WeDo models and Scratch games [here](https://github.com/HSSBoston/early). 😄 I still have a soft spot for boxes full of Lego; my dream is to buy every Lego set in the world and build my own little empire!

Later, I came across [Squeak Smalltalk](https://squeak.org/) and then Python, which helped me turn curious ideas into real projects, including a [computerized origami model](https://github.com/HSSBoston/jimmy-in-the-box), a [smart earring](https://github.com/HSSBoston/smart-earring/), a [sensor-equipped skateboard](https://github.com/HSSBoston/skateboard), an [underwater camera for observing a fish tank](https://github.com/HSSBoston/underwater-eye), and a [smart water bottle for hydration tracking and reminders](https://github.com/HSSBoston/sip-log). Somewhere along the way, coding became more than a way to make gadgets move, light up, sense, track, and respond. It became a bridge connecting the different parts of my life—science, art, sports, and technology—instead of keeping them in separate little boxes.

That bridge became especially important as my interests expanded into piano, tennis, science, and economics. These interests have led me to interdisciplinary projects such as [astronomical sonification](https://github.com/HSSBoston/astro-audio-ar), which combines coding, music, and astronomy to make complex visual information understandable through sound.  I have also developed a [probabilistic tennis metric](https://github.com/HSSBoston/tennis-stats) that uses coding and statistics to summarize and predict a player’s overall performance, as well as a [microeconomic math model](https://github.com/HSSBoston/econ-pets) that computationally explores how households balance the companionship, care, time, and costs in pet ownership. What excites me most is using technology and quantitative thinking to connect fields that may seem unrelated and to explore questions that cannot be answered from only one perspective.

You can find all my projects in [my GitHub repositories](https://github.com/HSSBoston?tab=repositories) and a few of them at [my Devpost portfolio](https://devpost.com/HSSBoston). I have completed Harvard University’s [CS50P](https://cs50.harvard.edu/certificates/5bcfd884-c335-4890-8340-8c73658a0e29) and [CS50T](https://courses.edx.org/certificates/b50eb41b15ce49508ec52c2eb52a4472), as well as Girls Who Code's summer program on data science and AI. I have also earned the Python Institute’s associate-level [PCAP](https://verify.openedg.org/?id=5bbh.0ER9.RGyM) certification.
